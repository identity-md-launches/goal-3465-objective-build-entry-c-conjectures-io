## Tables and polynomials

RV32IM uses integer high-half products, generated by fixed-width 64-bit
multiplications. `ex8_r` combines 256 32-bit high words and 256 16-bit low
words for an odd-anchor exponential table (1,536 bytes). Its actual indexing
uses 256 entries and a denominator of 512; older comments mentioning
`exp9_r`/512 entries describe an earlier layout. `nxq_r` has 161 cubic
records (2,576 bytes): negative exp inputs around [-4, -1/4] use 32 cells
per binade below magnitude 2 and 64 cells for magnitudes 2 to 4. `nq4_r`
has five arrays of 32 coefficients (640 bytes) for quartics around [-8, -4].
`tnl_r` has 25 anchors (100 bytes) for [-1/4, -2^-8], reducing to an anchor
rounded to three significant bits and applying a short residual expansion.

The activation kernels share tanh-based cells and anchors. `tld_r` contains
128 cubic deficit records (2,048 bytes); `tl_r` has 96 cubic records (1,536
bytes); `ta_r` has 138 cubic records (2,208 bytes); and `tla_r` contains
33 high/tail anchors (264 bytes). Deficit form returns the input word minus
a small rounded correction. `tb_r` contains 64 cubic negative-sigmoid
records (1,024 bytes), and `sq_r` contains 33 quartics (660 bytes). `tsr_r`
has 82 small-tanh thresholds (328 bytes). Small tanh/sigmoid ranges also
use odd-power series through degree seven, or the linear term with a cubic
and quintic correction where the narrower range permits it.

Thumb uses 16-bit partial products to avoid costly general widening multiply
on Cortex-M0+. `t_eps` packs 728 16-byte records (11,648 bytes), combining
activation cubic fields with auxiliary words used for the 512-entry exp
table, small-tanh thresholds and saturation cutoffs. `t_exp_lo` supplies
512 16-bit low words (1,024 bytes). `t_tw` has 80 reversed cubic deficit
records (960 bytes) shared by small tanh and sigmoid. `t_crows` has 144
three-word cubic records (1,728 bytes) for negative sigmoid. Tiny shift,
sign, threshold, pointer and plateau tables complete the emitted tables.
The source also declares unused fifth-degree `t_sm12` rows and `t_pdrow`
rows; they are absent from the emitted object symbol tables and are not
active scoring paths. `object-census.json` gives the compiled accounting,
including padding and ARM exception-index sections separately.

## Range reduction and fast paths

RV exp's general path estimates an odd anchor index with fixed-point
reciprocals of ln(2), reduces by multiples of ln(2)/512, loads the 256-entry
odd-anchor table, and approximates exp of the small residual quadratically.
The precise residual path retains more fractional bits and cubic/quartic
terms. Thumb exp uses a 512-entry table and a floor-style reduction by
ln(2)/512, with sign-specific biased indices that fold constants into the
remainder and exponent. A short quadratic residual and split products
produce the common result; the cold path adds precision as needed.

Sign/exponent/word tests lead directly to tiny-input identities, linear or
short-series paths, saturation plateaus, exp overflow/subnormal/underflow
paths, infinities and payload-preserving quiet NaNs. RV exp gives typical
negative inputs dedicated cubic/quartic cells before general reduction.
Sigmoid reuses tanh(x/2), including shared small-range residual decisions.
Thumb has explicit plateau cutoffs for positive sigmoid and large tanh,
and separate normal negative-sigmoid cells before the deep exp-based path.

## Hard cases

Fast results keep guard bits. Each path accepts values outside a narrow
midpoint window; rejected windows use a function/range-specific residual
comparison rather than a multiprecision incumbent fallback. RV activation
resolvers compare a candidate midpoint using a high/tail anchor and the
tanh addition identity. RV exp reconstructs only the modular window around
the midpoint, with tuned table low words replacing an older exception list.
Thumb activation resolvers use sum-form exponential identities, split products
and a small-range Pade/series decision. Thumb exp's residual has a final
32-bit perfect-hash mask for its 47 most ambiguous words. Explicit exceptional
input tests and formulae near overflow/subnormal thresholds also appear.
Comments claim exhaustive validation of fitted windows; those historical
claims are not new full-domain evidence from this run.

## Interpretation and next experiments

Quick coverage is partial. Neither a quick sanitizer check nor stratified
target samples certify all 2^32 inputs. Ratios printed by local evaluation
refer to `examples/incumbent.c`, not the current live champions. The manifest's
current-reference source requests returned HTTP 403; historical published
decision ratios are not same-seed current-champion comparisons.

Two future experiments, each in a new source and changing one technique:

1. Halve only RV's negative-exp cubic cell density for magnitudes in [2,4],
   from 64 to 32 cells per binade; refit cubic coefficients and validate the
   guard window across that range. Keep the dispatch and all other ranges
   unchanged. Measure the saved table/code size against increased midpoint
   resolver traffic on both seeds and new seeds, especially exp typical mean
   and hard p99.9. Exhaustively check the affected range before promotion.
2. Change only Thumb positive-sigmoid plateau lookup (`t_sig_plateau`) from
   its table-seeded linear cutoff scan to a balanced decision tree using the exact same
   `t_spcuts` values. Preserve all results and cutoffs. Compare Thumb v6-M
   wide/hard means and tails against extra code bytes; check every word in
   the affected plateau interval, then run the usual full-domain checks for
   any eventual candidate. Do not combine this with coefficient changes.
